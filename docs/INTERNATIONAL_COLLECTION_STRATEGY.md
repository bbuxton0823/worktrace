# International Collection Strategy

This document proposes an ethical, task-specific international expansion model for the WorkTrace prototype. It is an operating draft, not legal, labor, tax, immigration, insurance, privacy, or cross-border-transfer advice. Every country requires current local counsel and an accountable local operator before collection.

## Core decision

International collection should add useful task, object, environment, and work-style diversity. It should not be built around paying workers as little as possible.

The recommended sequence is:

1. Capture 20 buyer-reviewed accepted hours in a controlled United States staging or mock-site program.
2. Establish the buyer task specification, acceptance rules, rights manifest, privacy floor, and cost per accepted episode.
3. Select one country, one task family, one audited local partner, and one buyer-approved schema.
4. Capture 50 to 100 accepted hours and compare them blindly with the United States reference set.
5. Add sites inside the first country before adding another country.
6. Enter a second country only after quality parity, safe worker retention, complete rights evidence, and repeat buyer demand.

## Directional country-task hypotheses

These are pilot hypotheses. They are not confirmed wage, quality, demand, or legal conclusions.

| Market | Best first task candidates | Proposed role | Main caution |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mexico | Empty-home staging, furniture installation, retail handling, warehouse kitting | First nearshore candidate for rapid iteration with United States staging buyers | Confirm current private-sector privacy, labor, subcontracting, tax, site-rights, and transfer requirements |
| Colombia | Hospitality, furnished-rental turnover, retail, facilities work, Spanish-language QA | Strong nearshore service-work candidate | Structure international transfer or transmission roles country by country |
| India | Furniture assembly, facilities, hospitality, warehouse, controlled household-like tasks | Broad task-variety candidate with English-language coordination | Diligence partner labor practices, current DPDP implementation, competition, and home-context privacy |
| Philippines | Annotation and QA, plus tightly controlled hotel, laundry, or property-reset pilots | English-language quality-operations candidate | Obtain local advice on how NPC Circular 2025-01 applies before relying on reusable video |
| Vietnam | Furniture, garment, laundry, kitting, packaging, light industrial work | Specialist industrial collection market | Weaker match for United States home staging, with local-language and cross-border privacy design required |

“South America” is not one regulatory or labor market. Colombia, Brazil, Chile, Peru, and every other country require separate diligence, agreements, notices, wage benchmarks, and data-routing decisions.

## Current legal snapshot, July 2026

This section records the most important current planning issues found in primary regulatory sources. It is not a legal opinion, and the governing rules must be checked again immediately before recruitment, recording, or transfer.

### India

- India’s DPDP Act and final Rules are under phased commencement. The official November 2025 notification schedules the central processing, consent, rights, and transfer provisions 18 months after the notification date, approximately May 2027.
- Design to the future DPDP standard now, while also checking the Information Technology sensitive-personal-data rules and any other currently applicable law.
- First-person footage is personal information when a worker or bystander can be identified. Face, gait, hand geometry, or other analysis used to identify a person raises biometric risk.
- Commercializing worker video for robotics training should use a separate opt-in, image and performance release, data-license terms, and property authorization. Do not rely on ordinary employment administration as the purpose.
- India is the preferred Asia capture candidate for this plan, but an established employer, state-specific labor review, and current privacy review are required.

### Philippines

- The Data Privacy Act requires transparency, legitimate purpose, proportionality, security, accountability for international processing, and data-subject rights.
- NPC Circular 2025-01 expressly covers body-worn and similar recording devices used by other entities for training, quality control, monitoring, assessment, or evaluation.
- The regulator's public summary requires a lawful basis, notice, secure storage, access controls, masking, and responsive privacy and takedown procedures. The exact retention and reuse rules for this commercial worker-capture design must be confirmed from the full circular by Philippine counsel.
- A capture program should include a privacy impact assessment, visible notice, a recording indicator, encryption, access control, training, and secure deletion.
- Cross-border processing is possible with accountability controls, and the NPC publishes model contractual clauses. Start with annotation and QA while the capture, retention, and reuse questions are resolved.

### Vietnam

- Vietnam’s Law on Personal Data Protection No. 91/2025/QH15 and Decree 356/2025 took effect January 1, 2026.
- Consent is expected to be voluntary, informed, specific, verifiable, and separated by purpose. A commercial robotics-data program should not be presented as routine workforce monitoring.
- Images are personal data. Location, behavior tracking, biometrics, health, and private or family information receive heightened protection.
- Processing and cross-border impact-assessment duties can apply. The cross-border dossier generally must be submitted within 60 days of the first transfer, but local counsel must confirm the exact filing, role, and timing for the proposed structure.
- Begin with one controlled industrial or furniture site, keep raw footage in Vietnam, and export a reviewed derivative only after the required legal and impact work is complete.

### Mexico

- Mexico’s current private-sector law is the Federal Law on Protection of Personal Data Held by Private Parties, last amended November 14, 2025.
- Use a Spanish privacy notice and separate authenticated written consent covering recording, model-training and dataset-licensing purposes, retention, recipients, transfer terms, and ARCO procedures.
- A robotics buyer that determines its own training purposes is likely a separate controller rather than a simple processor. Make sale or license for robotics-model training a specific purpose and bind the recipient to the notice and transfer terms.
- Treat photographs or derived face, gait, or hand-geometry templates as sensitive biometrics when used to identify someone.
- If a staffing or staging partner supplies employees to third-party sites, confirm whether specialized-services and REPSE requirements apply. The current regulator is also reviewing modernization of the law, so re-check the framework at launch.

### Colombia

- Law 1581 of 2012 covers collecting, recording, storing, using, circulating, and deleting personal data. Colombian guidance treats video recording as intrusive personal-data processing.
- Obtain prior, express, informed authorization that can be proven later. Sensitive or identifying biometric use needs additional care.
- Distinguish an international transmission, where a processor acts only on a Colombian controller’s instructions, from a transfer to a robotics buyer that determines its own purposes.
- The SIC’s January 2026 list treated the United States and Mexico as countries with an adequate level of protection, but accountability, security, purpose limits, and contracts still apply. Verify the current list at the actual transfer date.
- Use an established employer partner and avoid contractor structures that function as controlled employment in practice.

## Fair-pay formula

```text
Total worker pay = base task pay
                 + paid capture overhead
                 + recording and data-rights premium
                 + reimbursed costs
                 + upside-only job-level quality bonus
```

- Base pay is the higher of the partner’s normal skilled-task rate or a locally benchmarked comparable-role rate.
- Setup, training, calibration, uploads, privacy pauses, troubleshooting, travel, and company-caused retakes are paid time.
- A separate recording premium makes the additional burden and data rights visible.
- A minimum shift payment protects workers when hardware, software, or site problems prevent accepted capture.
- Rejected media can never reduce base pay.
- Workers are not paid only by accepted minute, because that can pressure them to record private or unsafe moments.
- The property customer’s service discount never substitutes for worker compensation or consent.

Any percentage premium in an early budget is a hypothesis. Validate it through local benchmarks, worker interviews, local counsel, and partner payroll review before offering terms.

## Partner structure

| Party | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| WorkTrace | Buyer specification, field kit, application, task ontology, privacy rules, centralized QA, rights manifests, and payment audit |
| Local employer or service partner | Lawful hiring, payroll, required benefits, insurance, site permissions, safety, scheduling, and incident response |
| Independent auditor | Sample consent checks, payroll verification, confidential worker interviews, and privacy-control audits |
| Site partner | Controlled workspace and task access, without access to individual worker speed or acceptance data |

Contracts should prohibit recruitment fees, unauthorized subcontracting, productivity surveillance, retaliation for pausing or declining recording, and deductions tied to rejected data.

## Worker and site controls

- Start in vacant homes, mock rooms, training areas, warehouses, or controlled commercial sites.
- Record adults only. Exclude minors, residents, tenants, patients, and incidental bystanders.
- Disable audio, GPS, and precise property addresses by default.
- Provide a visible recording indicator, physical privacy control, delete-last-segment function, and safety stop.
- Provide notices and comprehension checks in the worker’s preferred language.
- Give workers a confidential grievance channel and a no-retaliation policy.
- Do not expose individual speed, efficiency, pause, rejection, or quality data to the employer.
- Pay for retakes caused by equipment, software, unclear instructions, or buyer changes.
- Use the same calibration objects and golden tasks in every country.
- Audit payroll receipts and interview a sample of workers independently.
- Keep service pricing, property authorization, worker consent, and downstream data licensing in separate documents.

## Region-first data architecture

```text
Wearable camera and phone
          |
Encrypted regional ingest
          |
Restricted regional raw vault
          |
Local privacy filtering and human QA
          |
Consent, site-rights, and transfer gate
          |
Approved task media, labels, tracks, and metadata
          |
Rights-scoped buyer package
```

Keep raw footage in a regional vault until country-specific review permits transfer. Prefer exporting privacy-cleared derivatives such as task segments, hand and object tracks, state labels, sensor telemetry, and outcome states. Export raw identifiable footage only when it is necessary, specifically authorized, contractually controlled, and lawful.

Every episode should carry:

- worker consent version, language, and jurisdiction;
- site authorization;
- permitted training, evaluation, and derivative uses;
- audio, location, face, document, and restricted-object scan results;
- export eligibility and country restrictions;
- retention and deletion status;
- revocation status;
- processor and delivery history;
- buyer release version.

## Quality parity test

Compare domestic and international samples without revealing country to the buyer reviewer. Measure:

- first-pass acceptance yield;
- hand and object visibility;
- timestamp and calibration quality;
- task and outcome coverage;
- state-transition accuracy;
- privacy and rights pass rate;
- worker-reported comfort and understanding;
- incident and deletion-request rate;
- cost per accepted episode;
- buyer ingestion success and measured model utility.

Scale by accepted task episodes, useful state transitions, object diversity, outcome coverage, and repeat purchase. Raw video hours alone are not the product.

## Subsidized service policy

Do not use free household service as a pretext to record economically vulnerable communities. If a subsidy is tested:

1. Use vacant properties or commercial operators first.
2. Pay full wages, capture overhead, and the recording premium.
3. Put service pricing and data rights in separate agreements.
4. Limit the subsidy by task, location, hours, and experiment duration.
5. Treat the subsidy as customer acquisition cost.
6. Stop if it does not lead to buyer-funded datasets.

The preferred model is a robotics buyer-funded specification and pilot. A discount can help secure a controlled site, but it should not become the permanent funding engine.

## Investor story

Position WorkTrace as a rights-clean, task-specific robotics data network, not a low-cost labor outsourcing company.

The potential defensible assets are:

- repeatable task specifications and ontology;
- audited worker and site-rights infrastructure;
- cross-country quality parity;
- comparable benchmark tasks;
- privacy-cleared multimodal derivatives;
- a fast buyer-specification-to-delivery workflow;
- repeat purchases and expansion into adjacent task packs.

Use customer-funded specifications and pilots first. Consider venture capital after one paid specification, one paid dataset pilot, evidence of repeat demand, known accepted-data economics, and successful one-country replication.
